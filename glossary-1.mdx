This glossary brings together every defined term across the GB flexibility market — from legally binding FMRs to operational NESO procurement documents to FMAR data model concepts. Each term is shown with its source so you can trace it back to where it carries authority.

**To find a term:** use the page search (top right) or jump to the source group below.

**Source groups:**

- **FMR-GLO** — Market Facilitator Glossary. Sections 1–3 are legally binding on System Operators via licence conditions.
- **Other FMRs** — local and rule-specific terms from the other Flexibility Market Rules: FMR-E2E (End-to-End Process), FMR-SBM (Standard Baselining), FMR-CRM (Carbon Reporting), FMR-PD (Product Definitions), FMR-PR (Primacy Rules), FMR-RSR (Revenue Stacking), FMR-VSM (Verification and Settlement).
- **FMAR Data Model** — concepts from the FMAR data dictionary that describe what FMAR will hold.
- **NESO procurement documents** — operational definitions from NESO's Balancing Services Glossary, Response, Balancing Reserve, and DFS Procurement Rules. Where terms recur across multiple documents with identical or near-identical definitions, they are shown once.
- **FGDs (Governance Documents)** — definitions from the Market Facilitator's Governance Documents: FGD-FMF (Rule Framework), FGD-CMP (Change Management Procedure), FGD-IMP (Implementation Monitoring Procedure).
- **CCS** — Consent and Credential Service domain.
- **DSI** — Data Sharing Infrastructure domain.
- **Industry / operational** — operationally common terms not yet formally defined in any FMR.
- **Device-level (SSES / PAS 1878)** — appliance-level definitions from the DESNZ Smart Secure Electricity Systems programme and PAS 1878:2026 (the British Standard for energy smart appliances).

A number of terms recur across multiple sources — sometimes with identical meaning, sometimes with subtle scope differences. The [**Overlapping terms across sources**](#overlapping-terms-across-sources) section below the tabs captures the cross-walk.

  <Tab title="Other FMRs">
    Local and rule-specific definitions from the Flexibility Market Rules outside of FMR-GLO. Each rule's terms are grouped into its own sub-section.

    ### FMR-E2E (End-to-End Process)

    Local definitions from the End-to-End Process Flexibility Market Rule. These describe the structural concepts of the FMR — Market Phases, Market Activities, and the Resource–Asset–Unit hierarchy.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Asset">
        A generalisation of DER (in FMR-PQC) and Eligible Asset (in NESO Service Terms) — relates to a logical concept to deliver the overall usable flexibility, involving Resources together with command and control, telecommunications, and local and/or remote energy management system. **An Asset represents one or more Resources that are controlled together at a single network connection point.** See [Assets: Overview](/assets/overview).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Service Provider (FSP) (E2E context)">
        The FMR-GLO global definition is "any person providing Flexibility Services". The End-to-End Process FMR adds context: "an umbrella term for the party who takes delivery and other contractual risks when providing Flexibility Services." See [Service Providers](/actors/service-providers).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Market Activity">
        A discrete, operational step carried out by a participant or operator that contributes to a broader market process. **Market Activity can occur in parallel or sequentially to one another.** See [Activity Dependencies](/lifecycle/activity-dependencies).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Market Phase">
        A logical grouping of Market Activities that share a common objective and temporal alignment in the end-to-end process. See [Lifecycle Phases](/lifecycle/phases).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Pre-Qualify">
        A process conducted ex-ante to determine whether a participant, asset, or unit meets the minimum technical, operational, commercial, and data-exchange requirements necessary to participate in a flexibility Sub-Market.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Qualification">
        The state of a Service Provider, Asset, or Unit being formally recognised as eligible and compliant to participate in a Sub-Market, having satisfied all applicable pre-qualification criteria and subsequently demonstrated compliance through verified performance. _Note: Qualification is also used to describe the Market Phase inclusive of the Activities needed to achieve the state of Qualification._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Resource">
        The physical Plant/Apparatus (BSC) or devices (e.g., Energy Smart Appliance/Customer Energy Manager (SSES)) that generate, consume or store electricity. **A Resource needs to be paired by an accountable Service Provider with an approved Metering System to become a registered Asset in a relevant Sub-Market.** See [Resources](/assets/resources).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Unit">
        A generalisation of Auction Unit (NESO) which may contain a single Asset or an Asset Group (DNO) to be used for a product procured via / delivered in a Sub-Market. See [Units](/assets/units).
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    ### FMR-SBM (Standard Baselining Methodologies)

    Defines the baselining methodologies used to assess flexibility delivery performance across DNO Sub-Markets.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Absolute % Error">
        Over an assessment period, the calculated average absolute error of a Meterable Unit.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Alternative Baselining Methodology (ABM)">
        A non-DBM baselining approach where the applicable DBM is not suitable due to the DER having atypical characteristics or operational behaviours.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Asset Capacity">
        A baselining technique which assumes the participating DER operates at its full rated capacity during the flexibility event, irrespective of actual operational conditions.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Confirmed Baselining Methodology (CBM)">
        The baselining methodology that will be utilised in the assessment of the performance of the DER. This will either be the relevant DBM, or, where approved by the DNO, the ABM.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Default Baselining Methodology (DBM)">
        The baselining methodology that should be applied based on the Location Type, Metering Location, Technology Type and direction of the service (i.e. DTD/GTU or DTU/GTD).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Distributed Energy Resource (DER)">
        Energy resources comprised of generation and/or storage and/or controllable load, which is connected at the low or medium voltage distribution level.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Event Days">
        A day that can be excluded from a Recent History baseline methodology for activities such as delivery of Balancing Services, Virtual Trading, Site Outage, Equipment and/or Site Disconnection occurring on that day.

        _Note: NESO's DFS Procurement Rules also use "Event Day" — defined in Schedule 3 of the DFS Procurement Rules. Closely related concept; the FMR-SBM definition is the substantive one for DNO Sub-Markets._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Fixed Reference">
        A baselining technique which uses a fixed daily profile derived from the observed energy behaviour of a representative group of DERs that represents normal operating behaviour or a DNO's planning scenarios.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Location Type">
        Either a domestic premise or an Industrial & Commercial (I&C) premise.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Meterable Unit">
        An aggregated collection of DERs, or a single DER, that are either all a non-Nomination DBM or all a Nomination DBM.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Metering Location">
        A location for the metering either at the Point of Connection to the Distribution System (i.e. the Boundary Point Metering System) or behind the Boundary Point Metering System located at the asset providing the flexibility service.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Net % Error">
        Over an assessment period, the calculated average net error of a Meterable Unit. A negative net error indicates Nominations are higher than the Meterable Unit's normal behaviour, while a positive error indicates the Nominations are under the Meterable Unit's normal behaviour.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Nomination">
        A baselining technique which uses an estimated/expected set of meter values, provided by the Flexibility Services Provider (FSP), based on the expected or normal operation of the site / DER without any flexibility event, for a future availability window that then becomes a utilisation event.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Recent History">
        A baselining technique which uses a set of recent, eligible days (i.e. excluding Event Days such as a day providing a flexibility service) preceding a utilisation event. This may also include correction factors such as within-day adjustments for weather variables or removal of outlier values.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Technology Type">
        The type of Distributed Energy Resource (DER) which is the asset providing the flexibility service. For example, an EV Charger.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Zero">
        A baselining technique where the baseline energy consumption or generation is set to zero, meaning no energy usage or production is assumed in the absence of the utilisation event.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    ### FMR-CRM (Carbon Reporting Methodology)

    Sets out the aligned approach to reporting on the carbon impact of flexibility services across DNO Sub-Markets.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Conversion factors">
        Used in this rule as a general term to describe factors that are used in the calculations which can include fuel emission factors, grid intensity factors, and plant efficiency assumptions.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Grid intensity">
        The carbon intensity of electricity that is imported from the grid in kgCO2e/kWh taking into account the carbon emissions from grid generation, interconnector imports, and potentially network losses.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Payback">
        Otherwise known as rebound or bounce back, refers to when demand side response load increases after a turn-down event as the site recovers to pre-event conditions. This could also refer to a similar rebound effect following realisation of economic benefits as a result of an energy efficiency saving measure.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Reporting Boundary">
        Defines the scope of the calculation. This can include direct impacts (such as combustion of fuel to generate electricity), consequential impacts (such as the carbon emissions from grid generation to produce and transport electricity imported by a battery), or indirect impacts (such as embedded and end of life emissions attributed to the assets). Narrow or wide boundaries refer to the extent to which the calculation focuses on direct or whole-life emissions respectively.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    ### FMR-PD (Product Definitions)

    Defines the standard flexibility Products procured across DNO Sub-Markets.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Continuous">
        A sustained delivery over the entire utilisation window.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Peak Delivery">
        Targeting the maximum response that can be delivered within the window.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    ### FMR-PR (Primacy Rules)

    Manages conflict between System Operators in the operation of the MW Dispatch Sub-Market.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="MW Dispatch">
        The Sub-Market of that name, operated by NESO.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    ### FMR-RSR (Revenue Stacking Requirements)

    Sets common definitions for revenue stacking across all System Operator Sub-Markets.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Co-delivery">
        A single asset receives multiple payments for using the same capacity, at the same time, in the same direction.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Jumping">
        A single asset receives multiple payments for services in different times (adjacent or non-adjacent).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Splitting">
        A single asset receives multiple payments for using different capacity, at the same time.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    ### FMR-VSM (Verification and Settlement)

    Operational terms for the verification of flexibility delivery and the settlement of payments under DNO flexibility contracts.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Accepted Availability Window">
        A Committed Availability Window in respect of a Flexible Unit which is notified by the Company in the form of an award as being required by the Company for the Company's Flexibility Services.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Availability Payment">
        Payment made by the Company to a Flexibility Service Provider for the period the Flexibility Service Provider was awarded an Availability Window.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Availability Percentage">
        The calculated percentage of monthly Availability Payment due, following the application of Utilisation Performance and any applicable availability Grace Factor.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Availability Performance">
        The overall monthly utilisation delivery performance calculated as a percentage for application to all availability payments at the point of invoicing.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Availability Price">
        The contracted fee £/MW/h in respect of availability.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Availability Refinement Period">
        The period defined within the flexibility service parameters where a refinement of the availability window and capacity is agreed.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Available MW">
        The MW value the Company has awarded the FSP within an Accepted Availability Window.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Baseline MW">
        The baseline value of the Flexible Unit at the minute in question. A Demand asset is represented by a negative number with a generation asset a positive number.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Contracted Capacity">
        The target net MW of Demand Response in respect of an awarded Service Window, confirmed to the FSP at time of award or following a refinement window (if applicable for the contracted flexibility service).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Delivered MW">
        The MW value calculated as delivered following the application of the Baseline MW to the Metered MW.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexible Unit">
        One or more Energy Resources that are aggregated, regarded as a single entity, and metered on a single metering output and, as such, is providing a flexibility service.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Grace Factor">
        The percentage of under delivery below 100% for which renumeration of 100% will be applied, relevant both to utilisation and availability performance at the Company's discretion.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Metered MW">
        The actual metered MW value of the Flexible Unit at the minute in question. A Demand asset is represented by a negative number with a generation asset a positive number.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Metered Time Period">
        The time period used in the payment calculations, this will either be minute by minute or 30-minute granularity.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Peak Reduction Service">
        A flexibility service where FSPs commit to reduce their highest demand peaks during pre-contracted windows.

        _Note: distinct from the FMR-GLO term "Peak Reduction (PR)" which refers to the Product. "Peak Reduction Service" here is the broader service category as it appears in FMR-VSM verification and settlement context._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Performance Multiplier">
        The factor by which any percentage of under delivery below the grace factor will be multiplied by in order to calculate payable delivery.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="The Company">
        The Licensee with whom the FSP is contracted to provide flexibility services.

        _Note: in NESO procurement contexts, this role is held by NESO/NGESO; in DNO Sub-Markets it is the relevant DNO. The term refers to the contracting Licensee in the specific Sub-Market context._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Turnup/Turndown Service">
        Flexibility services that require FSPs to change their energy import or export behaviour during pre-scheduled windows or in response to a Utilisation Instruction.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Utilisation Event or Utilisation Period">
        A utilisation request to deliver flexibility services, inclusive of a specified start and end time.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Utilisation Instruction">
        An instruction by the Company to the FSP to deliver Flexibility Services.

        _Note: closely related to the operational concept "Utilisation Signal" used in industry vocabulary. FMR-VSM uses "Utilisation Instruction" specifically in the verification and settlement context._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Utilisation Payment">
        The amount payable by the Company to the Provider for the utilisation of any Flexibility Service.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Utilisation Performance">
        The calculated performance per utilisation event after any factors have been applied.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Utilisation Price">
        The contracted fee £/MWh in respect of utilisation.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Utilised MW">
        The MW value instructed within a Utilisation Event. Represented by a positive number for a demand reducer / generation increase asset and a negative number for a demand increase / generation reduction asset.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

  <Tab title="FMAR Data Model">
    Concepts from the FMAR data dictionary describing the entities and attributes that FMAR will hold. These define the data model the FMAR system of record is built around.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Asset Level Metering Point">
        Metering equipment that measures Imports and/or Exports for individual assets behind the Boundary Metering Point. It includes BSC Asset Metering Systems, and other non-BSC asset meters.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Asset Qualification">
        The record, of an approval of an Asset Registration, by a System Operator, to enable its utilisation within a Flexibility Market for which they are responsible.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Boundary Level Metering Point">
        The point at which a supply to (Export) or from (Import) a Distribution Network and Premises is measured.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Connecting System Operator (CSO)">
        Licensed companies responsible for network operations at point of common coupling. The Role held by a DNO in respect of a network connection on its network. See [Master Conceptual Map](/master-conceptual-map).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Consumer">
        The person or organisation who owns the Technical Resources which can be controlled to deliver Flexibility Services.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Distribution Network">
        The Distribution Network to which the Flexibility Asset is connected.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Distribution Network Connection">
        The area within the Distribution Network where a Boundary Level Metering Point is located.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Asset">
        One or many Technical Resources, which can be controlled and dispatched, by a Flexibility Service Provider, as a single entity; measured by a single Flexibility Metering Arrangement.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Asset Registration">
        The record of a Flexibility Asset, created by an FSP, requesting that asset's qualification for utilisation within a Flexibility Market operated by an SO.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Asset Relationship">
        The type of association that an FSP has to a specific Flexibility Asset. The FSP can define their relationship as one of Owner, Operator or Aggregator.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Market Participant Role">
        A Flexibility Organisation performing a specific Flexibility Market Role.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Market Role">
        A defined role which can be performed within the Flexibility Market.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Metering Arrangement">
        The means by which a specific Flexibility Asset is measured via meters installed at a Boundary Level Metering Point and/or an Asset Level Metering Point.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Organisation">
        A registered company operating within the Flexibility Market.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Flexibility Technical Resource">
        The physical Plant/Apparatus or devices i.e., Energy Smart Appliance/Customer Energy Manager that generates, consumes or stores electricity.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="FMAR Administrator">
        Governance entity handling adjudications and technical system administration.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="FSP Qualification">
        The authorisation of an FSP, by an SO, to enter Flexibility Markets which the SO is responsible for.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="FSP Registration">
        The request by an FSP, to an SO, to become qualified to operate within Flexibility Markets which the SO is responsible for.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Grid Supply Point (GSP)">
        The physical connection point where the national transmission system connects to a regional distribution network.

        _Note: NESO procurement documents reference Grid Supply Point as defined in the Grid Code or BSC; FMAR uses an FMAR-design-specific operational definition._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="GSP Group ID">
        Identifies one of the 14 designated areas within Great Britain where the Distribution Network is located.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Premises (FMAR)">
        The place where energy is consumed or generated by a Consumer.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Procuring System Operator (PSO)">
        Licensed companies procuring flexibility services (e.g. NESO, DNOs in their procurement role). The Role of being the buying party in a Sub-Market. See [Master Conceptual Map](/master-conceptual-map).
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Transmission Network Connection">
        The connection point between a transmission network and a distribution network.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Unit Qualification">
        The approval of a Flexibility Unit, by an SO, for its utilisation in a specific Flexibility Product.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

  <Tab title="NESO procurement">
    Operational definitions from NESO's procurement documentation. NESO publishes:

    - A **Balancing Services Glossary of General Terms and Rules of Interpretation** — cross-cutting definitions applying to all NESO Balancing Services contracts. Most other NESO procurement documents reference back to it.
    - Per-product **Procurement Rules** documents — Response, Balancing Reserve (BR), Quick Reserve (QR), Slow Reserve (SR), Demand Flexibility Service (DFS) — each containing rules for that specific procurement.
    - Corresponding **Service Terms** documents containing the contractual terms for each service.

    This tab groups terms by source. Where a term recurs across multiple NESO procurement documents with identical or near-identical wording, it appears once with all sources noted. Where definitions diverge meaningfully, separate entries are shown.

    ### Auction mechanics (common to Response, BR, QR, SR Procurement Rules)

    These terms appear across NESO's day-ahead auction procurements with identical or near-identical definitions. The Auction is the core mechanism for procuring Response, Balancing Reserve, Quick Reserve, and Slow Reserve.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Acceptance Ratio">
        For any Order, a number between zero (0) and one (1), indicating the proportion of an Offered Quantity or a Bid Quantity which has been matched by the Auction algorithm.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Accepted">
        In respect of any Order, where its Acceptance Ratio is greater than zero (0). A Sell Order shall be Partially Accepted if its Acceptance Ratio is less than one (1).

        _Aliases: Acceptance._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Active Network Management Scheme">
        A scheme operated by a Public Distribution System Operator which is designed to continually monitor in real time constraints on an area of the network for the purpose of allocation of available capacity.

        _Used identically in DFS, Response, and BR Procurement Rules._
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Agent (NESO)">
        In respect of any Registered Auction Participant or Registered BR Participant, its agent (if any) notified to NESO pursuant to the Registration and Pre-Qualification Procedure.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction">
        In respect of all Auction Products and Service Windows in a Service Day, the operation of the algorithm pursuant to the Market Clearing Rules and the submission of Orders pursuant thereto.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction Administrator">
        The operator from time to time of the Designated Auction Platform, which may be NESO.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction Clearing Quantity">
        In respect of any Auction Product and for each Service Window falling in any Service Day, the sum of the Contracted Quantities.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction Closing Time">
        The time on D-1 (the day before the Service Day) by which Sell Orders must be submitted. **14:00 hours** on the EFA Day immediately preceding the Service Day for Response Auctions; **14:00 hours** on the Service Day immediately preceding the relevant Service Day for BR Auctions.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction Opening Time">
        **08:00 hours on D-15** for both Response and BR Auctions, where D is the Service Day.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction Product">
        Any Response Product (in the Response Procurement Rules) or any Balancing Reserve Product (in the BR Procurement Rules), or any QR/SR Auction Product as relevant.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Auction Results Time">
        The time on D-1 by which Auction results are notified to participants. **16:00 hours** for Response Auctions; **14:30 hours** for BR Auctions.
      </Accordion>

      <Accordion title="Basket">

## Overlapping terms across sources

A number of terms appear in multiple sources, sometimes with identical meaning, sometimes with subtle scope differences, and occasionally with genuinely conflicting definitions. This section captures the cross-walk so colleagues can see which definition applies in which context.

### Aligned overlaps — same concept, no material difference

These terms are defined in multiple sources but with the same essential meaning. Differences are stylistic or refer to the same legal entity from different angles.

| Term | Sources | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Authority** | FMR-GLO; NESO procurement (Balancing Services Glossary); DESNZ SSES | All three resolve to the Gas and Electricity Markets Authority (Ofgem). FMR-GLO is the cleanest definition; NESO BSG carries legacy phrasing referencing the Director General of Electricity Supply; DESNZ SSES is concise. |
| **Balancing and Settlement Code (BSC)** | NESO procurement; DESNZ SSES | NESO points to the Transmission Licence; DESNZ SSES references condition E1 of the Electricity System Operator Licence. Same code. |
| **Connection and Use of System Code (CUSC)** | NESO procurement; DESNZ SSES | NESO defines via the Secretary of State designation; DESNZ SSES describes the contractual framework for NETS. Same code. |
| **Distribution Code** | NESO procurement; DESNZ SSES | NESO references "drawn up by Public Distribution System Operators pursuant to Distribution Licences"; DESNZ SSES describes the technical parameters in the DCUSA context. Same Code, different framing. |
| **Grid Code** | NESO procurement; DESNZ SSES | NESO defines via the Transmission Licence; DESNZ SSES describes it as the technical rules for connecting to NETS. Same Code. |
| **National Energy System Operator (NESO)** | FMR-GLO; DESNZ SSES; NESO procurement (as NGESO — same legal entity, pre-Oct-2024 name) | All three refer to NESO Limited (company number 11014226). |
| **Working Day** | FMR-GLO ("banks open in London"); NESO procurement BSG ("Operational Day Mon–Sat 05:00, excluding bank holidays") | These two definitions differ. The FMR-GLO version is calendar-based; NESO BSG is operational-day-based. **In contracts, check which definition the contract incorporates.** |
| **Implementation Date** | FMR-GLO; FGD-CMP | The FGD-CMP definition is the elaborated source — referencing consultation in the Change Management Procedure, Authority appeal direction, and any Derogation issued. The FMR-GLO version is the summary. |
| **Industry Code** | FMR-GLO; FGD-CMP | Same definition expressed slightly differently. FGD-CMP adds: "Reference to 'Industry Code' shall include all codes subject to this definition". |
| **Publish Date** | FMR-GLO; FGD-CMP | FGD-CMP is the elaborated source. Both refer to the date an Approved Solution takes legal effect. |

### Contextual variants — same concept, scope differs

These terms refer to the same underlying concept but each source defines it for a specific purpose. The differences are intentional and reflect what each document needs from the term.

| Term | Sources | Where they diverge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Consumer** | FMAR Data Model; CCS; DESNZ SSES; PAS 1878 | FMAR: owner of Technical Resources controllable to deliver flexibility. CCS: data subject who grants consent. DESNZ SSES: a consumer in relation to electricity conveyed by distribution / transmission systems. PAS 1878: domestic / small business user with authority to enter into a DSR service contract. **Four overlapping but distinct framings — pick the one matching your context.** |
| **Consumer (CCS) vs Customer (Energy Manager) (PAS)** | CCS; PAS 1878 | The CCS Consumer is the data subject. The PAS CEM is the _system that manages_ the consumer's appliances. Distinct entities, both relate to the same household. |
| **Distribution Network / DNO** | FMR-GLO ("party which holds a Distribution Licence"); FMAR Data Model ("the Distribution Network to which the Flexibility Asset is connected"); DESNZ SSES ("network or operator authorised to be operated by the holder of an electricity distribution licence") | FMR-GLO defines the licensed entity; FMAR Data Model defines the network as a connection target; DESNZ SSES bundles network and operator. **Different layers of the same concept.** |
| **Energy Smart Appliance (ESA)** | DESNZ SSES; PAS 1878 | DESNZ SSES is regulatory/licensing-flavoured ("appliance capable of adjusting flow of electricity in response to a load control signal"); PAS 1878 is technical-standard-flavoured ("communications-enabled, able to respond to price/other signals by shifting/modulating consumption/production"). Same kind of device, different framings. |
| **Flexibility Service Provider (FSP)** | FMR-GLO ("any person providing Flexibility Services"); FMR-E2E ("party who takes delivery and other contractual risks"); DESNZ SSES ("organisation entering into a contract with a consumer to provide for the sending of a load control signal") | FMR-GLO is broadest; FMR-E2E adds the risk-bearing characteristic; DESNZ SSES is narrowest, scoping FSP specifically to consumer-load-control contracts in the SSES regulatory framework. **DESNZ SSES uses FSP differently — narrower.** |
| **Grid Supply Point (GSP)** | FMAR Data Model ("physical connection point where transmission connects to distribution"); NESO procurement (defined per BSC or Grid Code) | FMAR uses an operational definition; NESO procurement defers to existing industry codes. Same physical concept. |
| **Premises** | FMAR Data Model ("place where energy is consumed or generated by a Consumer"); NESO procurement (DFS context: "any land, building or structure to which a supply of electricity is made"); PAS 1878 ("geospatial extent owned or occupied by a consumer and containing one or more appliances") | FMAR is consumer-centric; NESO DFS is supply-point-centric; PAS is geospatial/appliance-centric. **All three are usable for the same physical place; the framing differs.** |

### Conceptual cousins — distinct but easily confused

These terms aren't quite the same concept but get conflated in conversation. Worth distinguishing.

| Terms | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| **FSP (FMR-GLO/E2E) vs Flexibility Service Provider (DESNZ SSES) vs Demand Side Response Service Provider (PAS 1878 — DSRSP)** | The FMR FSP is broad — anyone providing flexibility services. The DESNZ SSES FSP is narrowed to consumer-load-control contracts. The PAS DSRSP is "an organization using ESAs to provide demand-side energy management services to regulated electricity market participants". The PAS DSRSP is closer to a subset of FMR FSP — specifically the ESA-aggregator subset. **Three concentric scopes for related-but-distinct roles.** |
| **Resource (FMR-E2E) vs appliance (PAS 1878) vs Energy Smart Appliance (PAS / SSES)** | FMR Resource is a broad category — "Plant/Apparatus or devices that generate, consume or store electricity", explicitly including ESAs. PAS appliance is a generic product/system that consumes/stores/generates. PAS/SSES ESA is the smart subset. **FMR Resource is the umbrella; ESA and appliance are sub-categories.** |
| **Interoperability (DESNZ SSES) vs interoperability (PAS 1878)** | DESNZ SSES defines it specifically as "the ability of the ESA to change its FSP without the need for a visit to the premises and whilst maintaining the ability to provide CLF". PAS 1878 defines it as "the ability of an ESA to work seamlessly across any appropriate DSR service operated by any authorized system player, including allowing a consumer to switch an ESA to a different DSRSP at any time and maintain DSR functionality". **Both about FSP/DSRSP switching; PAS is broader (DSR services beyond CLF).** |
| **Customer Energy Manager (CEM, PAS 1878) vs Homes Energy Management System (HEMS, DESNZ SSES)** | PAS CEM: logical entity managing one or more ESAs to provide DSR services, can be in-premises or cloud. DESNZ HEMS: device or system controlling/configuring energy usage of one or more ESAs to optimise across all devices in a consumer premises. **PAS CEM is the standardised technical concept; HEMS is a regulatory-context-specific term that overlaps heavily with CEM.** |
| **Smart Meter (PAS 1878) vs Boundary Meter (NESO DFS)** | PAS smart meter: device measuring electrical energy transfer per the prevailing national smart meter spec (SMETS2). NESO Boundary Meter: a Meter comprised in a Boundary Point Metering System. **Both can be the same physical device in many cases**, but the framings serve different purposes — settlement metering vs smart metering specification compliance. |
| **Peak Reduction (FMR-GLO Product) vs Peak Reduction Service (FMR-VSM)** | FMR-GLO defines **Peak Reduction (PR)** as a specific named Product (procured under FMR-PD). FMR-VSM defines **Peak Reduction Service** more broadly as a service category where FSPs commit to reduce highest demand peaks during pre-contracted windows. **The Product is a specific instance; the Service is the broader category.** |
| **Utilisation Instruction (FMR-VSM) vs Utilisation Signal (industry)** | FMR-VSM's **Utilisation Instruction** is the formal contractual term — "an instruction by the Company to the FSP to deliver Flexibility Services". The operational shorthand **Utilisation Signal** appears in industry usage to mean the same thing — the actual signal that initiates delivery. Use Utilisation Instruction in formal contexts. |
| **Distributed Energy Resource (DER, FMR-SBM) vs Resource (FMR-E2E) vs Eligible Asset (NESO Service Terms)** | FMR-E2E **Resource** is the broadest umbrella — physical Plant/Apparatus or devices that generate, consume, or store electricity. FMR-SBM **DER** is narrower: specifically generation/storage/controllable load connected at the low or medium voltage distribution level. NESO **Eligible Asset** is narrower again: any Plant and Apparatus pre-qualified to provide a specific NESO Auction Product. **Three concentric scopes — DER is the FMR-SBM term used for DNO Sub-Markets; Eligible Asset is the NESO-specific term.** |
| **Event Days (FMR-SBM) vs Event Day (NESO DFS)** | FMR-SBM defines **Event Days** as days excluded from a Recent History baseline methodology. NESO DFS Procurement Rules has an **Event Day** entry pointing to Schedule 3 of the DFS Procurement Rules. **Both are about days excluded from baseline calculation; FMR-SBM is the substantive cross-Sub-Market definition.** |
| Flexible Unit (FMR-VSM) vs Meterable Unit (FMR-SBM) vs Unit (FMR-E2E) |  |

### Reading guide

For colleagues using this glossary, three rules of thumb:

1. **For legal/contractual context, prefer FMR-GLO** where defined. It is the legally binding cross-cutting glossary.
2. **For operational specifics within a Sub-Market, use that Sub-Market's own document.** NESO Response Procurement Rules will tell you exactly what "Acceptance Ratio" means in a Response Auction; don't import a different definition.
3. **For SSES / consumer-load-control discussions, use DESNZ SSES and PAS 1878 verbatim** — they carry distinct regulatory and technical framings that don't translate cleanly into FMR vocabulary.

Where you spot a definition that contradicts another in a problematic way, raise it with the Market Facilitator — over time, FMR-GLO is intended to grow to cover more of these terms and reduce reliance on Local definitions.

## A note on terminology evolution

The FMR-GLO is a living document. Version 1.0 was published 12 December 2025 and includes a relatively small set of Global definitions; the explicit intent is to grow this set over time and reduce reliance on Local definitions across individual FMRs.

NESO procurement documents (DFS Procurement Rules, Response Procurement Rules, BR Procurement Rules, etc.) and DNO Service Terms continue to maintain their own definitions for operational clarity. Over time, terms that recur across procurement contexts may migrate into FMR-GLO; until then, both sets of definitions coexist on this page.

NESO's own documentation continues to use **NGESO** (National Grid Electricity System Operator Limited) throughout — this is the same legal entity (company number 11014226) that became NESO in October 2024 under the Energy Act 2023.
