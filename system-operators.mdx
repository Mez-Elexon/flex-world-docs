The Market Facilitator Glossary (FMR-GLO) defines **System Operator** precisely:

> _Entities that are responsible for the operation and coordination of electricity systems and markets. Specifically, this includes: National Energy System Operator (NESO) and Distribution Network Operators (DNOs)._

System Operators are the buy side of the flexibility market. They identify needs, define Products, and procure flexibility from FSPs.

## NESO

The **National Energy System Operator** is defined in FMR-GLO as "National Energy System Operator Limited (company number: 11014226) (and any successor to its role) established under the Energy Act 2023."

NESO became a public corporation in October 2024, taking on the system operator function previously held within National Grid plc.

**What NESO does in flexibility markets:**

- Identifies national-level system needs (frequency response, reserve, balancing, constraint management).
- Specifies Products under NESO Service Terms.
- Procures flexibility through ancillary service auctions, dedicated platforms, and the Balancing Mechanism.
- Coordinates with DNOs on whole-system issues.

**Sub-Markets NESO operates** include the Response, Reserve, Constraint Management, Reactive, and Stability families, plus the Balancing Mechanism. See [Sub-Market Catalogue](/reference/sub-market-catalogue) for the full list.

## DNOs

A **Distribution Network Operator** is defined in FMR-GLO as "a party which holds a Distribution Licence in respect of distribution activities in Great Britain, acting in that capacity." A Distribution Licence is one issued under section 6(1)(c) of the Electricity Act 1989, **excluding** licences issued to Independent Distribution Network Operators (IDNOs).

The IDNO exclusion is structural: IDNOs are licensed but separately defined and not currently in scope as System Operators for FMR purposes.

There are six DNO groups in GB:

| DNO Group | Licence Areas |
| --- | --- |
| UK Power Networks (UKPN) | LPN (London), SPN (South Eastern), EPN (Eastern) |
| National Grid Electricity Distribution (NGED, formerly WPD) | SWALES (South Wales), SWEST (South West), EMID (East Midlands), WMID (West Midlands) |
| Northern Powergrid (NPg) | NPgY (Yorkshire), NPgN (North East) |
| SP Energy Networks (SPEN) | SP Distribution, SP Manweb |
| SSEN | SSES (Southern), SSEH (Scottish Hydro) |
| Electricity North West (SPENWL — confusingly close to SPEN) | SP ENW (North West) |

**What DNOs do in flexibility markets:**

- Identify local network needs, typically in Constraint Managed Zones.
- Publish flexibility tenders, often via market platforms.
- Pre-qualify Assets and Units at the network level (grid connection, technical capability, grid impact).
- Dispatch contracted flexibility when constraints arise.
- Verify delivery and settle payments.

Each DNO runs multiple Sub-Markets across the named Products (Operational Utilisation, Peak Reduction, Scheduled Availability \+ Operational Utilisation, Scheduled Utilisation, Variable Availability \+ Operational Utilisation), with variations on time period and license area. The full list is at [Sub-Market Catalogue](/reference/sub-market-catalogue).

A note on **DSO**: many DNOs increasingly describe themselves as Distribution System Operators (DSOs), reflecting an active rather than passive network role. This is a positioning rather than a regulatory category — for FMR purposes the licensed entity is still the DNO.

## NESO–DNO coordination

A central design challenge of the GB flexibility market is that the same flexibility, behind the same connection point, may be useful to both NESO and a DNO. Coordinating procurement and dispatch so that:

- the same Asset/Unit isn't double-counted,
- conflicting instructions aren't issued,
- value is fairly allocated,

is the focus of significant ongoing work. The Market Facilitator's coordination role and the FMAR programme are both centrally concerned with this challenge.
