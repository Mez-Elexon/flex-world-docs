A **Resource** is the physical thing that delivers flexibility. The End-to-End Process FMR defines it as:

> _The physical Plant/Apparatus (BSC) or devices (e.g., Energy Smart Appliance/Customer Energy Manager (SSES)) that generate, consume or store electricity. A Resource needs to be paired by an accountable Service Provider with an approved Metering System to become a registered Asset in a relevant Sub-Market._

This definition deliberately spans:

- **BSC-style "Plant and Apparatus"** — the long-standing wholesale market vocabulary for generation and large-scale flexible plant.
- **SSES-style "Energy Smart Appliances and Customer Energy Managers"** — the consumer-side vocabulary used in the Smart Secure Energy System programme.

By defining Resource broadly, the FMRs accommodate everything from a 200 MW battery storage site to a smart EV charger in a domestic garage.

## What can be a Resource

Examples of Resources currently active in or relevant to the GB flexibility market:

| Class | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Storage** | Battery energy storage systems (BESS), pumped hydro, thermal storage |
| **Generation** | Distributed generators, on-site CHP, diesel/gas reciprocating engines |
| **Demand-side flexible load** | Industrial process loads, HVAC systems, refrigeration |
| **Domestic flexible load** | Heat pumps, EV chargers, hot water cylinders, smart appliances |
| **Vehicles and V2X** | Electric vehicles connected via charge points capable of bidirectional flow |
| **Energy management systems** | Customer Energy Managers and aggregating control systems treated as Resources where they exercise the controllability |

A Resource doesn't have to _only_ generate or _only_ consume — many modern Resources are bidirectional (batteries, V2G chargers).

## When does a Resource become "in the market"?

Three conditions, per the End-to-End Process FMR definition:

1. **An accountable Service Provider takes responsibility for it.** The FSP signs the contractual chain that makes the Resource's flexibility commercially exposable. Without an FSP, a Resource is just hardware.
2. **It is paired with an approved Metering System.** Performance has to be measurable to a standard the relevant Sub-Market accepts. The metering route varies — settlement metering, sub-metering, or telemetry-based measurement — but it must be approved.
3. **It is included in a registered Asset.** The Resource doesn't enter the market on its own; it enters as part of an Asset registration. See [Assets](/assets/assets).

Until all three are in place, the Resource is invisible to the market.

## Resources and aggregation

Most Resources don't participate alone. Aggregation happens at two levels:

- **Within an Asset.** Multiple Resources at one connection point are typically registered as one Asset (e.g. a site with both a battery and an EV bank behind one MPAN).
- **Within a Unit.** Multiple Assets, possibly from different connection points, can be combined into one bidding Unit (e.g. an FSP's portfolio of 10,000 heat pumps).

The aggregation pattern depends on the Product being targeted and the Sub-Market's rules. A Resource doesn't decide its own aggregation — that's set by how it's registered upstream.

## Telemetry and control

A Resource that is to deliver flexibility under instruction must be reachable. The communication path typically involves:

- A **control gateway** at or near the Resource — battery management system, EV charger, customer energy manager, building management system.
- A **communication channel** between that gateway and the FSP's systems — proprietary, OpenADR-based, or via standardised APIs.
- A **dispatch path** from the FSP back to the System Operator, completing the loop.

The Resource itself doesn't always know that it's "in the flexibility market" — much of the intelligence lives in the FSP's energy management layer.

## Resource lifecycle considerations

Resources have lifecycle events independent of their Asset registration:

- **Installation and commissioning.**
- **Firmware and software updates.**
- **Capability degradation** (e.g. battery state of health declining over years).
- **Replacement, often part-by-part.**
- **Decommissioning.**

These events may or may not require an Asset re-registration depending on their materiality. Minor firmware updates typically don't; replacing a major component or significantly changing capability usually does.
